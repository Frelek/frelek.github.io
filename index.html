uRANS vs DES vs LES Turbulence Showdown

Deep Stall & Unsteady Vortex Dynamics over NACA 2412 at Re = 6.85 × 10⁶ (30° AoA)

🔬 The Physics Objective

When an airfoil operates deep in post-stall regime (30° AoA) at high Reynolds number (\(Re = 6.85 \times 10^6\)), which turbulence modeling approach provides physically truthful predictions of separated vortex shedding, pressure pulsations, and energy dissipation?

I performed a direct computational showdown across three paradigms in ANSYS Fluent:

  • uRANS (Unsteady RANS): Time-averaged Navier-Stokes equations with SST \(k\text{-}\omega\) closure. Highly economical, captures bulk separation cycles, but filters out high-frequency spectral turbulent cascades.
  • DES (Detached Eddy Simulation): Hybrid RANS-LES approach. Retains robust wall-boundary treatment with RANS and activates 3D Large Eddy Simulation in massive separation zones.
  • LES (Large Eddy Simulation): Resolves the 3D filtered Navier-Stokes equations directly down to the spatial subgrid scale (WALE / Smagorinsky), capturing Kelvin-Helmholtz roll-ups and inertial subrange energy cascade.

🧪 Experimental Benchmarking & Modeling Strategy

Impinging gas jets represent one of the most demanding benchmarks in aerothermodynamics, combining stagnation pressure surges, intense wall-jet shearing, and localized peak convective heat transfer rates critical for turbine blade cooling and rocket engine thrust-chamber thermal management.

To simulate a truly two-dimensional impinging configuration within ANSYS CFX (which operates natively in 3D), I engineered a single-cell-wide pseudo-2D computational domain with symmetry/periodic boundary conditions. I authored a custom User-Defined Function (UDF) in C to prescribe fully developed turbulent pipe flow velocity profiles at the nozzle exit, ensuring deterministic inflow boundary conditions.

📊 Key Findings & Heat Transfer Insights

  • Turbulence Model Divergence: Benchmarked SST \(k\text{-}\omega\) against \(k\text{-}\epsilon\) formulations, discovering that standard eddy viscosity models overpredict turbulence kinetic energy at the stagnation point without Kato-Launder corrections.
  • Nusselt Number Distribution: Mapped local wall heat transfer coefficients and Nusselt number distributions against published experimental laser-Doppler velocimetry (LDV) benchmarks, achieving tight correlation within the wall-jet acceleration zone.
  • Mesh Refinement at Stagnation Point: Demonstrated that local Body of Influence (BOI) cell density is paramount to preventing artificial numerical diffusion in the jet shear layer.

✈️ Commercial Turbofan Compressor & Turbine Blade Aeromechanics

During my tenure at the Engineering Design Center (EDC) / Warsaw Institute of Aviation partnering with GE Aerospace, I evaluated rotating turbomachinery components across commercial jet engine compressors and turbines. My core responsibility was preventing aeromechanical flutter and mitigating high cycle fatigue (HCF) caused by unsteady aerodynamic forcing.

📈 Campbell Diagram Analysis

Constructed multi-order Campbell diagrams across engine operating speed sweeps (idle to redline RPM). Identified dangerous resonance crossings where bladed-disk natural frequencies coincided with aerodynamic excitation harmonics (engine order lines, stator-vane wake passing frequencies, and inlet total pressure distortion wakes). Guided mass distribution and chordwise stiffness tuning to clear resonance bands without compromising aerodynamic pressure ratios.

🔬 Modal Analysis & High Cycle Fatigue (HCF)

Conducted 3D finite element modal analyses across flexural, torsional, and stripe modes utilizing temperature-dependent alloy properties. Constructed modified Goodman diagrams to evaluate alternating vibratory stress amplitudes against steady centrifugal and aerothermal mean stresses. Correlated analytical predictions against full-scale engine test data from dynamic strain-gauges and blade tip-timing (BTT) optical probes.

🚀 Computational Aerodynamics of Starship Reentry

Conducted an extensive computational investigation on the aerodynamic behavior of the SpaceX Starship vehicle during atmospheric entry. The research analyzed how the blunt forward body, actuated forward canards, and aft body flaps interact to control vehicle pitch, lift, drag, and descent attitude throughout high-angle-of-attack belly-flop maneuvers.

📊 15.5-Million-Cell Mesh Discretization

Engineered a 15.5-million-cell computational domain in ANSYS Fluent utilizing tailored polyhedral-prism volume grids. Placed targeted Bodies of Influence (BOI) around canard hinge gaps and flap deflection zones to capture shock detachment, leeward recirculation, and crossflow boundary-layer separation while enforcing \(y^+ \approx 1.0\).

⚙️ Superposition Principle for Control Surfaces

  • Deflection Matrix: Simulated canards and aft flaps under independent and counterposition deflections across pitch, yaw, and roll attitudes.
  • Superposition Verification: Proved that the aerodynamic forces and moments of the forward canards and aft body flaps can be linearly superimposed with minimal cross-coupling error, saving over 60% in computational expenditure.
  • Aerodynamic Database (ADB): Provided an aerodynamic look-up database and automated Python interpolation scripts for 6-DOF trajectory simulation and entry corridor targeting.

🚀 Reusable Booster Atmospheric Guidance

My Bachelor's thesis, titled "Aerodynamic Performance Analysis of Falcon 9 Grid Fins: A Computational Investigation", examined the fluid dynamics governing SpaceX's titanium grid fins during first-stage booster atmospheric descent.

Lattice grid fins offer superior control authority with minimal hinge torque compared to planar fins, yet present severe shock-choking challenges in the transonic corridor.

🔬 CFD Modeling & Shockwave Physics

  • Domain Formulations: Built 2D planar lattices and full 3D body-mounted booster stages with symmetry planes to capture wing-body aerodynamic interference.
  • Transonic Choking Phenomena: Captured the formation of detached normal shock waves in front of individual lattice cells as the internal flow chokes near Mach 1.0, generating a steep wave drag surge.
  • Wave Drag Delay via Back-Swept Geometry: Demonstrated how SpaceX's back-swept titanium grid fin design maintains attached oblique shocks, delaying normal shock detachment beyond Mach 1.5.

🪐 Project Summary

This Master's Thesis validated laminar aerothermodynamic models for the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) forebody using ANSYS Fluent, calibrated against Caltech T5 shock‑tube heat‑flux data. I learned the full set of theoretical formulations and governing equations (compressible Navier‑Stokes, multi‑species mass, vibrational energy, and chemical source terms) and explored how mesh resolution, 1‑T vs 2‑T energy formulations, and surface catalysis affect peak heating, informing TPS design for Martian entry.

During atmospheric entry into Mars at hypervelocities ranging from \(6.0\text{ to }8.5\text{ km/s}\), a spacecraft's kinetic energy is converted into extreme internal and thermal energy across a detached hypersonic bow shock. In the Martian atmosphere (\(\sim 97.1\%\text{ CO}_2, 2.9\%\text{ N}_2\)), temperatures behind the shock wave exceed \(6,000\text{–}10,000\text{ K}\), triggering strong thermal non-equilibrium (vibrational excitation relaxation) and chemical non-equilibrium (molecular dissociation of \(CO_2\) into \(CO, C, O,\) and \(O_2\)).

This Master's Thesis presents a comprehensive numerical validation of laminar aerothermodynamic modeling for the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) 70° sphere-cone forebody aeroshell. Simulations were carried out using ANSYS Fluent and rigorously validated against experimental surface heat flux measurements from the Caltech T5 Hypervelocity Shock Tunnel (Shot 2254), originally conducted and published by Wright et al. (NASA Ames / Caltech).

The investigation explores the coupled sensitivities of spatial discretization accuracy (First-Order vs Second-Order Upwind schemes), thermal state (One-Temperature 1T vs Park Two-Temperature 2T formulation), and surface catalycity boundary conditions (Non-Catalytic, Mitcheltree finite-rate catalytic recombination, Supercatalytic, and Adiabatic wall conditions), mapping out the complete aerothermal heating envelope for Martian entry TPS sizing.

📐 Theoretical Formulations & Governing Aerothermodynamic Equations

The hypersonic flowfield is governed by the compressible Navier-Stokes equations extended with multi-species mass conservation, vibrational energy conservation, and chemical source terms:

Park Two-Temperature (2T) Energy Model Thermal Non-Equilibrium
\[ \frac{\partial (\rho e_v)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho e_v \mathbf{u}) = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{q}_v + Q_{T-V} + \sum_s \dot{\omega}_s e_{v,s} \]
Coupled conservation of vibrational energy \(e_v(T_v)\), driven by translational-vibrational energy exchange \(Q_{T-V}\) and dissociation enthalpy sink terms.
Landau-Teller Vibrational Relaxation Energy Relaxation
\[ Q_{T-V} = \sum_s \rho_s \frac{e_{v,s}^*(T) - e_{v,s}}{\tau_s}, \quad \tau_s = \tau_s^{MW} + \tau_s^P \]
Millikan-White relaxation time with Park high-temperature cross-section correction preventing unphysically short relaxation times at \(T > 5,000\text{ K}\).
Park Geometric Controlling Temperature Chemical Dissociation
\[ T_a = \sqrt{T \cdot T_v} \quad \left(T_a = T^q T_v^{1-q}, \; q = 0.5\right) \]
Geometric mean temperature used in modified Arrhenius rate coefficients \(k_{f,r}(T_a)\), capturing the delay of chemical dissociation until vibrational modes are excited.
Total Surface Convective Heat Flux Boundary Condition
\[ q_w = -\kappa \left( \frac{\partial T}{\partial n} \right)_w - \kappa_v \left( \frac{\partial T_v}{\partial n} \right)_w - \sum_{s=1}^{N_s} J_{s,w} h_{s,w} \]
Sum of translational-rotational conduction, vibrational energy conduction, and diffusive species enthalpy transport driven by heterogeneous wall catalysis.

⚗️ 5-Species Carbon Dioxide Reaction Mechanism

The chemical kinetic mechanism comprises 5 neutral species (\(CO_2, CO, C, O, O_2\)) undergoing 5 forward and reverse elementary reactions:

$$1.\quad \mathrm{CO}_2 + M \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CO} + \mathrm{O} + M \quad (\Delta H_{298}^\circ = +532.2\text{ kJ/mol})$$
$$2.\quad \mathrm{CO} + M \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{C} + \mathrm{O} + M \quad (\Delta H_{298}^\circ = +1076.4\text{ kJ/mol})$$
$$3.\quad \mathrm{O}_2 + M \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{O} + M \quad (\Delta H_{298}^\circ = +498.4\text{ kJ/mol})$$
$$4.\quad \mathrm{CO} + \mathrm{O} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{C} + \mathrm{O}_2 \quad (\Delta H_{298}^\circ = +578.0\text{ kJ/mol})$$
$$5.\quad \mathrm{CO}_2 + \mathrm{O} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CO} + \mathrm{O}_2 \quad (\Delta H_{298}^\circ = +33.8\text{ kJ/mol})$$

🛡️ Surface Catalysis Boundary Formulations

  • Non-Catalytic Wall (NC): Chemically inert surface with zero species mass flux (\(J_{s,w} = 0\)). Dissociated atoms cannot recombine at the wall, yielding the theoretical lower bound for aerodynamic heating (\(q_{\text{stag}} \approx 620\text{--}662\text{ W/cm}^2\)).
  • Mitcheltree Diffusion-Limited Catalytic Model: Heterogeneous Eley-Rideal / Langmuir-Hinshelwood recombination model formulated for Martian heat shields: \[ O + s \to O_s, \quad CO + s \to CO_s, \quad CO_s + O_s \to CO_2 + 2s \] Recombines carbon monoxide and atomic oxygen into carbon dioxide at a rate constrained by diffusion through the boundary layer (\(q_{\text{stag}} \approx 712\text{--}739\text{ W/cm}^2\)).
  • Supercatalytic Wall (SC): Idealized fully catalytic wall forcing mass fractions to their freestream / fully recombined equilibrium state (\(Y_{CO_2} = 1.0\)), releasing all chemical dissociation enthalpy directly into the vehicle surface (\(q_{\text{stag}} \approx 1125\text{--}1140\text{ W/cm}^2\)).
  • Adiabatic Wall: Zero heat transfer condition (\(q_w = 0\)) to compute the stagnation recovery temperature \(T_{aw} \approx 6,612\text{ K}\).

🔑 Key Scientific Conclusions from the Thesis

  • Superiority of Mitcheltree Catalysis: The Mitcheltree diffusion-limited recombination model under the 2T-SS scheme (\(q_{\text{stag}} = 738.7\text{ W/cm}^2\)) demonstrates remarkable agreement with the experimental T5 thermocouple measurements across the entire spherical nose cap (\(r/R_b \le 0.5\)) and along the conical flank.
  • Catalycity Bounding Limits: The Supercatalytic assumption overpredicts stagnation heating by over 54.3% (\(1,140.1\text{ W/cm}^2\)), while the Non-Catalytic assumption underpredicts by 10.3% (\(662.3\text{ W/cm}^2\)). This conclusively demonstrates that assuming full catalycity imposes an overly severe mass penalty on Thermal Protection Systems, while non-catalytic assumptions would lead to catastrophic vehicle burn-through.
  • Impact of Vibrational Non-Equilibrium (2T vs 1T): Incorporating Park's 2T vibrational energy equation increases the predicted stagnation heat flux by approximately 3.7% and sharpens the bow shock standoff distance, as vibrational freezing delays energy partition into translation.
  • Spatial Discretization Order: First-order upwind schemes introduce artificial numerical diffusion that thickens the shock layer and rounds off the peak heating at the stagnation point and shoulder corner. Second-order spatial accuracy is mandatory for flight-accurate TPS sizing.

📋 Governing Assumptions in Fay-Riddell Aeroheating Computations (Master's Thesis pp. 20–23) [1][8]:
1. Axisymmetric Stagnation Streamline [1]: Steady, laminar boundary layer at the stagnation point (\(x = 0\)) of a blunt sphere-cone aeroshell forebody.
2. Normal Shock Wave Rankine-Hugoniot Jump [6]: Flow across the detached bow shock is treated locally as a normal shock wave, yielding post-shock edge conditions (\(p_e, T_e, \rho_e\)).
3. Modified Newtonian Velocity Gradient [6][8]: Flow acceleration outside the boundary layer is governed by Newtonian impact theory: \(\left(\frac{du_e}{dx}\right)_s = \frac{1}{R_n}\sqrt{\frac{2(p_e - P_\infty)}{\rho_e}}\).
4. Constant Boundary-Layer Pressure [1]: Boundary layer normal momentum reduces to \(\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} = 0 \implies p_w \approx p_e\) across the thin viscous layer.
5. Isothermal Cold Wall (\(T_w = 300\text{ K}\)) [8]: Heat shield surface is maintained at cold temperature (\(T_w \ll T_e\)), maximizing the driving enthalpy gradient (\(h_{0e} - h_w\)). Wall density obeys \(\rho_w = p_e / (R_{\text{gas}} T_w)\).
6. Sutherland Viscosity Law [7]: Dynamic transport properties \(\mu_e\) and \(\mu_w\) are governed by Sutherland's temperature-dependent formulation.
7. Constant Boundary-Layer Prandtl Number [1][8]: \(Pr = \frac{\mu c_p}{\kappa}\) is assumed uniform across the stagnation boundary layer (\(0.70\) for Martian \(\text{CO}_2\), \(0.71\) for Earth Air).
8. Lewis Number & Catalysis Parameterization [1][8]: Species mass diffusion is parameterized by \(Le = \frac{\rho D_{12} c_p}{\kappa}\). Dissociation recombination is handled via the thesis catalysis regimes: Equilibrium (\(Le^{0.52}\)), Frozen Catalytic Wall (\(Le^{0.63}\)), Frozen Non-Catalytic (\(1 - h_D/h_{0e}\)), or Convective (\(Le \approx 1\)).

Atmospheric State & Viscosity [5, 7] Step 1: Ambient Fluid
$$\rho = \frac{P(z)}{R_{\text{gas}} \cdot T(z)}, \quad \mu(T) = \mu_0 \left(\frac{T}{T_0}\right)^{3/2} \left(\frac{T_0 + S}{T + S}\right), \quad \nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho}$$

Extracts ambient temperature \(T(z)\) and static pressure \(P(z)\) across altitude layers \(z\) (US Standard Atmosphere 1976 [5] or Martian CO₂ model), computes fluid density via the ideal gas law, and applies Sutherland's law [7] to yield dynamic viscosity \(\mu\) and kinematic viscosity \(\nu\).

\(\mu\)Dynamic viscosity [Pa·s]

Chord Reynolds Number (Re_L) [3, 4] Step 2: Boundary Layer
$$Re_L = \frac{U_\infty \cdot L}{\nu}$$

Governs the ratio of dynamic inertial forces to viscous forces across characteristic length \(L\) [3][4]. Dictates whether the boundary layer is laminar or turbulent (\(Re_{\text{crit}} \approx 5 \times 10^5\)).

\(Re_L\)Reynolds number based on length \(L\) [-]

$$C_f = \begin{cases} \dfrac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_L}} & (Re_L < 5\times 10^5 \text{ — Blasius [2]}) \\[8pt] 0.0592 \cdot Re_L^{-0.2} & (Re_L \ge 5\times 10^5 \text{ — Schlichting [3]}) \end{cases}$$

Computes the dimensionless skin friction coefficient from the Blasius exact laminar boundary-layer solution [2] or the Schlichting-Gersten / Frank White turbulent correlation [3][4].

\(C_f\)Skin friction coefficient [-]
\(Re_L\)Local characteristic Reynolds number [-]
\(Re_{\text{crit}}\)Transition threshold (\(5 \times 10^5\)) [3]
Boundary Layer Thickness (δ_99) [2, 3, 4] Step 3: Viscous Layer
$$\delta_{99} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{5.0 \cdot L}{\sqrt{Re_L}} & (Re_L < 5\times 10^5 \text{ — Laminar [2, 3]}) \\[8pt] 0.37 \cdot L \cdot Re_L^{-0.2} & (Re_L \ge 5\times 10^5 \text{ — Turbulent [3, 4]}) \end{cases}$$

Determines the physical normal distance from the solid wall where tangential flow velocity recovers to \(99\%\) of freestream (\(u = 0.99 U_\infty\)), guiding overall prism layer refinement extent.

\(\delta_{99}\)Boundary layer thickness [m / mm]
\(L\)Characteristic body length / chord [m]
\(Re_L\)Characteristic Reynolds number [-]
Wall Shear Stress (τ_w) & u_τ [4] Step 4: Near-Wall Scaling
$$\tau_w = \frac{1}{2} \rho U_\infty^2 C_f \quad \Longrightarrow \quad u_\tau = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_w}{\rho}} = U_\infty \sqrt{\frac{C_f}{2}}$$

Quantifies the tangential viscous shear force per unit area at the wall and defines the characteristic friction velocity scale \(u_\tau\) fundamental to the law of the wall [4].

\(\tau_w\)Wall shear stress [Pa] (\(\text{N/m}^2\))
\(u_\tau\)Friction velocity scale [m/s]
\(\rho\)Fluid density [kg/m³]

Near-Wall Mesh Resolution (y⁺, Δy) [4] Step 5: CFD Meshing

Calculates the exact first prism inflation cell height \(\Delta y\) required to resolve the laminar viscous sublayer (\(y^+ \approx 1\)) for low-Reynolds CFD turbulence models (\(k\text{-}\omega\) SST, Spalart-Allmaras) [4].

7 Formulations ▼
Speed of Sound (a_∞) & Flight Speed (V_∞) [6] Step 1: Gas Dynamics
$$a_\infty = \sqrt{\gamma R_{\text{gas}} T_\infty}, \quad V_\infty = M_\infty \cdot a_\infty$$

Computes freestream acoustic speed \(a_\infty\) from ambient atmospheric temperature \(T_\infty\) and gas ratio of specific heats \(\gamma\), establishing flight velocity \(V_\infty\) for incoming Mach number \(M_\infty\) [6].

\(a_\infty\)Freestream acoustic speed of sound [m/s]
\(V_\infty\)True hypersonic flight entry velocity [m/s]
\(\gamma\)Ratio of specific heats (1.40 for Air, 1.30 for CO₂)
\(M_\infty\)Flight Mach number [-]
Rankine-Hugoniot Normal Shock Jumps [6] Step 2: Shock Layer
$$\frac{p_e}{p_\infty} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1}\left(M_\infty^2 - 1\right), \quad \frac{\rho_e}{\rho_\infty} = \frac{(\gamma + 1)M_\infty^2}{2 + (\gamma - 1)M_\infty^2}, \quad T_e = T_\infty \left(\frac{p_e}{p_\infty}\right) \left(\frac{\rho_\infty}{\rho_e}\right)$$

Governs conservation of mass, normal momentum, and total energy across the detached bow shock along the stagnation streamline, delivering post-shock boundary layer edge state (\(p_e, T_e, \rho_e\)) [6].

\(p_e, p_\infty\)Post-shock edge and freestream static pressure [Pa]
\(\rho_e, \rho_\infty\)Post-shock edge and freestream density [kg/m³]
\(T_e, T_\infty\)Post-shock edge and freestream static temperature [K]
\(M_\infty\)Hypersonic flight Mach number
Isothermal Cold Wall State (ρ_w, p_w) [8] Step 3: Wall State
$$p_w \approx p_e, \quad \rho_w = \frac{p_w}{R_{\text{gas}} \cdot T_w} = \frac{p_e}{R_{\text{gas}} \cdot T_w}$$

In accordance with boundary-layer momentum (\(\partial p / \partial y = 0\)), wall pressure equals post-shock edge pressure \(p_e\). Evaluates wall fluid density \(\rho_w\) at isothermal cold-wall temperature (\(T_w = 300\text{ K}\)) [8].

\(p_w\)Wall pressure (\(p_w \approx p_e\)) [Pa]
\(\rho_w\)Wall fluid density [kg/m³]
\(T_w\)Cold wall isothermal temperature (Default: \(300\text{ K}\)) [K]
\(R_{\text{gas}}\)Specific gas constant [J/(kg·K)]
Sutherland Dynamic Viscosities (μ_e, μ_w) [7, 8] Step 4: Transport
$$\mu(T) = \mu_0 \left(\frac{T}{T_0}\right)^{3/2} \left( \frac{T_0 + S}{T + S} \right) \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \mu_e = \mu(T_e), \quad \mu_w = \mu(T_w)$$

Computes temperature-dependent dynamic viscosity at the hot boundary-layer edge (\(\mu_e\)) and cold isothermal wall (\(\mu_w\)) for Air (\(S = 110.4\text{ K}\)) and \(\text{CO}_2\) (\(S = 222.0\text{ K}\)) [7][8].

\(\mu_e\)Dynamic viscosity at post-shock edge temperature \(T_e\) [Pa·s]
\(\mu_w\)Dynamic viscosity at cold wall temperature \(T_w\) [Pa·s]
\(\mu_0, T_0, S\)Sutherland transport calibration parameters [7]
Newtonian Stagnation Velocity Gradient [6, 8] Step 5: Shock Layer
$$\left(\frac{du_e}{dx}\right)_s = \frac{1}{R_n} \sqrt{\frac{2(p_e - P_\infty)}{\rho_e}}$$

Calculates the streamline acceleration away from the stagnation point (\(x = 0\)) for a spherical nose of radius \(R_n\) via Newtonian flow impact theory (Master's Thesis Eq. 9, p. 21) [6][8].

\(\left(\frac{du_e}{dx}\right)_s\)Newtonian velocity gradient [s⁻¹]
\(R_n\)Nose radius of curvature [m] (\(0.04445\text{ m}\) for MSL model)
\(p_e - P_\infty\)Pressure drop across stagnation streamline [Pa]
\(\rho_e\)Boundary layer edge density [kg/m³]
Stagnation & Wall Enthalpy Potentials [6, 8] Step 6: Thermodynamics
$$h_{0e} = c_p T_\infty + \frac{1}{2}V_\infty^2, \quad h_w = c_p T_w, \quad \Delta h = h_{0e} - h_w \quad \left(c_p = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}R_{\text{gas}}\right)$$

Quantifies the total stagnation enthalpy \(h_{0e}\) entering the shock layer and the wall enthalpy \(h_w\), defining the thermodynamic enthalpy driving potential \(\Delta h = h_{0e} - h_w\) that powers conductive/diffusive heat transfer [6][8].

\(h_{0e}\)Total freestream stagnation enthalpy [J/kg / MJ/kg]
\(h_w\)Wall gas enthalpy at \(T_w\) [J/kg / MJ/kg]
\(\Delta h\)Driving enthalpy potential across the boundary layer [J/kg]
\(c_p\)Isobaric specific heat capacity [J/(kg·K)]
Rayleigh Pitot Total Pressure Ratio [6] Step 9: Shock Entropy Loss
$$\frac{P_{02}}{P_{01}} = \left[ \frac{(\gamma+1)M_\infty^2}{2 + (\gamma-1)M_\infty^2} \right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} \cdot \left[ \frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma M_\infty^2 - (\gamma-1)} \right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}$$

Computes total pressure recovery across the normal shock wave, quantifying aerodynamic stagnation pressure retention and entropy generation at hypersonic flight speeds [6].

\(P_{02} / P_{01}\)Ratio of post-shock to pre-shock total pressure [-]
\(M_\infty\)Flight Mach number [-]
\(\gamma\)Ratio of specific heats [-]

$$\Phi_{\text{cat}} = \begin{cases} 1 + \left(Le^{0.52} - 1\right)\dfrac{h_D}{h_{0e}} & \text{Equilibrium Boundary Layer (Thesis Eq. 5 [1, 8])} \\[10pt] 1 + \left(Le^{0.63} - 1\right)\dfrac{h_D}{h_{0e}} & \text{Frozen Boundary Layer + Fully Catalytic Wall (Thesis Eq. 7 [1, 8])} \\[10pt] 1 - \dfrac{h_D}{h_{0e}} & \text{Frozen Boundary Layer + Non-Catalytic Wall (Thesis Eq. 8 [1, 8])} \\[10pt] 1.0 & \text{Convective Simplification } (Le \approx 1.0) \text{ (Thesis p. 23 [8])} \end{cases}$$

Future Research Horizons

Emerging Methodologies in Rarefied Gas Dynamics & Computational Physics

🌌 DSMC (Direct Simulation Monte Carlo)

Simulating transitional and free-molecular hypersonic regimes where the continuum hypothesis breaks down (\(Kn > 0.05\)). Tracking stochastic collision dynamics of billions of gas molecules during upper-atmosphere orbital entry.

🔄 Hybrid CFD-DSMC Multiscale Solvers

Coupling Navier-Stokes continuum solvers in dense shock layers with DSMC particles in rarefied outer flow domains, maximizing computational speed without sacrificing molecular-scale physics.

⚛️ Kinetic Boltzmann Solvers

Discrete velocity method (DVM) solvers integrating the non-linear Boltzmann transport equation to eliminate statistical Monte Carlo noise in micro-fluidic and low-speed rarefied regimes.

🧠 Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

Embedding Navier-Stokes and boundary-layer conservation laws directly into deep neural network loss functions for real-time aerodynamic coefficient inference during spacecraft entry guidance.
